Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961
Deduction in respect of life insurance premia, deferred annuity, contributions to provident fund, subscription to certain equity shares or debentures, etc.

An individual or Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) can claim a deduction under this section for payments made towards:
- Life insurance premium
- Contributions to Public Provident Fund (PPF)
- Employee's contribution to recognized Provident Fund
- Subscription to National Savings Certificates (NSC)
- Investment in ELSS (Equity Linked Savings Scheme)
- Principal repayment of home loan
- Tuition fees for children's education (maximum 2 children)
- Fixed deposits with scheduled banks (5-year term)
- Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (SCSS)
- Sukanya Samriddhi Account

The maximum deduction allowed under Section 80C is Rs. 1,50,000 per financial year.

Section 24 of the Income Tax Act, 1961
Deductions from income from house property

Interest on borrowed capital:
Where the property has been acquired, constructed, repaired, renewed or reconstructed with borrowed capital, the amount of any interest payable on such capital shall be deducted.

For self-occupied property:
- The deduction for interest paid on home loan is allowed up to Rs. 2,00,000 per annum for self-occupied residential property.
- This limit applies to interest paid during the financial year.

For let-out property:
- There is no upper limit for deduction of interest in case of let-out property.
- The entire interest paid during the year can be claimed as deduction.

Pre-construction period interest:
Interest paid during the pre-construction period (before acquisition/construction is complete) is allowed as deduction in 5 equal installments starting from the year in which the property is acquired or construction is completed.
